## 1. Why this test exists

The seven-screen Rejoice Always Challenge already works as a **hardcoded Local Prototype**. BerryMark v0.1 must eventually reproduce its **intended, reviewed content structure** through a Sanity import without silently deleting fields, changing theology, or exposing metadata.

A test must distinguish the following **three genuinely different sources**:

1. **Local Prototype:** The existing hardcoded `nov-2` Challenge; source is the Bibleberry TypeScript file.
2. **Sanity synthetic fixture:** Existing development-only placeholder data. Not the seven-screen Challenge.
3. **BerryMark → Sanity draft:** The eventual one-way importer output. **Does not exist yet.**

Never infer that two screenshots came from the same source because their titles and visual styles match.

## 2. Development-only visible identification

When a protected development preview is implemented, require **one unmistakable diagnostic banner on every screen**, for example:

```text
BERRYMARK TEST  ·  DEV ONLY
Source: BerryMark → Sanity draft
Document: 2026-11-02-rejoice-always
BerryMark revision: <short commit>
Screen: 3 of 7 · rejoicing-is-not-pretending
```

An optional **Show block IDs** mode should mark the rendered block boundary with the *actual* block type and ID, e.g. `CB8 · study-jesus-and-grief`. For repeated elements, show nested IDs as appropriate.

**These annotations are generated by the protected developer UI**, not authored inside Alexis's manuscript/BerryMark text and not stored in reader-facing Sanity fields. Never ship the debug banner in the public reader.

For synthetic fixtures, distinct sentinel strings such as `BM-TEST-CB4-LEFT`, `BM-TEST-CB4-RIGHT`, and `BM-TEST-CB9-PRAYER` may be useful; don't add such strings to the real manuscript adaptation.

## 3. Important unresolved preview architecture

The existing Sanity CMS Lab fetches from `src/cms/sanity/readPublishedChallenges.ts` using the **`published` perspective**. It explicitly excludes drafts, internal review notes and reviewer identities. Therefore a newly imported BerryMark **draft will not automatically appear** in that CMS catalog.

**Required future design decision:** Provide a **separate protected developer draft-preview mechanism**, authorized server-side, distinct from the public published-reader path. A browser-visible privileged Sanity token, temporary publication, disabling review gates, or leaking confidential drafts through a publicly readable dataset are **not acceptable substitutes**.

Until that preview path exists, a valid importer can create a Sanity draft and return a Studio edit link, but cannot truthfully claim the complete draft was rendered in the local Sanity CMS Lab. The existing Local Prototype may be compared visually, but it is not proof that BerryMark compiled correctly.

## 6. Negative tests: what must **not** happen

- **No copied NIV passage text** until rights are confirmed. The current Local Prototype **does** supply the NIV verse and configured memorization; the BerryMark draft intentionally does not. Treat this as a documented, safety-motivated difference, not a random regression.
- No invented `series`, `sequence`, `releaseAt`, reviewer, approval timestamp, or URL.
- No `EDITORIAL NOTE`, `TODO`, `REVIEW REQUIRED`, or `BM-TEST` marker appearing inside Alexis's reader-facing teaching.
- No automatic editorial approval, auto-publish, or public availability merely from import.
- No review-required visual adaptation silently promoted to approved.
- No optional deeper study, reflection or companion item counted as a completion requirement.
- No loss of the two prototype `pullQuote` presentations. The proposed mapping to separate CB3 statements needs direct inspection.
- No re-import over an existing draft/publication with the same stable identity.
- No browser-visible privileged Sanity token or public leak of draft provenance.

## 7. Source-fidelity and behavior audit

A successful structural render does **not** establish theological/editorial approval. Specifically inspect:

- The defined distinction between joy as feeling and rejoicing as posture/practice.
- Acknowledgment of suffering and Jesus weeping.
- The Gospel, atonement, reconciliation, redemption and future promise.
- Whether the CB5/CB6 adaptations preserve the argument rather than reducing it to slogans.
- Whether the two optional studies in screen 3 and the optional study in screen 5 are accurate and suitable for Alexis's approval.
- Whether the two reflection options preserve the original wording and pastoral sense.
- The exact concluding refrain and authored prayer.
- The source order of `And that's where our rejoicing comes from.` differs from the placement in the Local Prototype; this existing difference must be reviewed, not covered up during migration.
